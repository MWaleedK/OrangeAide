The Adventures of
DUMPY DIMPLE
And his DOG!

[Illustration: Dumpy Dimple, dressed in hat, thick coat and scarf, smiles and holds Rag who looks lovingly up to Dumpy’s face]

Pictured by G. F. Christie.

Told by M. Byron.

NEW YORK HODDER & STOUGHTON.

[Illustration: THOMAS FORMAN & SONS PRINTERS

NOTTINGHAM ENGLAND.]

This is a story, short and simple,
About the adventures of Dumpy Dimple,
And Rag, his dog. They were very queer,
And most exciting,--as you shall hear!

No doubt your friends may have talked to you
Of the wonderful battle of Bunkerloo,
And the things that happened to Dumpy D.--
Oh! don’t I wish they would happen to me!

How he hunted beasts of the fiercest kind,
How he fought each enemy he could find,--
How he captured a regiment single-handed,
And they had to do just as he commanded.

Dumpy Dimple was short and fat,
But extremely brave in spite of that;
And Rag, the pluckiest dog was he,--
They were both as bold as bold can be.

And they made a laager--a camp, you know,--
With sacks and boxes all in a row,--
They’d a gun, a sword, and a flag to wave,--
No wonder they were so bold and brave!

And Dick, the horse (of the four-wheel breed),
He could snort and gallop--he could, indeed!
In fact, they were just the sort, all three,
For having adventures--as you will see!

The First Adventure.

[Illustration: Rag peeks out of the top of a box surrounded by a sack and spade lying in the grass]

THEY DISCOVER THE ENEMY AND RAG GOES SCOUTING!

Said, Dumpy Dimple, “Now, Rag, stand steady:
Is the rifle loaded? Is everything ready?
The enemy’s coming soon, I hope,--
I see him, I think, through my telescope.

[Illustration: Dumpy and Rag stand atop the box, Dumpy looking afar through a telescope to the trees, with sack, spade, hobby horse and umbrella nearby]

So you had better be off to scout,--
Go very quietly, creep about,--
But if you should hear the rifle crack,
Quick as you can, dear Rag, come back!”

The Second Adventure.

[Illustration: Elephant mounted on platform with wheels laying on its side]

THEY STALK A MONSTROUS ELEPHANT.

Just then a trumpet began to blow,
And Dumpy whispered, “I told you so!”
A fearful Elephant, huge, immense,
Was glaring over the garden-fence!

[Illustration: Dumpy and Rag hide in the bushes while watching the elephant looking over the fence]

They stalked the Elephant inch by inch,--
He was dreadfully big--but they didn’t flinch:
Dumpy, choosing the proper spots,
Brought him down in a couple of shots.

The Third Adventure.

[Illustration: Camp fire beneath a kettle hanging from wooden poles held together at the top]

THEY MAKE TEA.

Dumpy and Rag were as pleased as Punch,
But both of them felt it was time for lunch:
A nice camp-table they managed to fix,
And boiled the kettle on blazing sticks.

[Illustration: Rag sits on tree stump with paws on a makeshift table on which are placed a jar of jam, bread and a knife, while Dumpy pours water from the kettle into a mug]

For however brave a hero may be,
He’s braver far when he’s had some tea:
And Rag was ever so fond of a crust,
Though he wouldn’t touch if you told him--“Trust!”

The Fourth Adventure.

[Illustration: Savage Bunny on skateboard]

THEY GO A-HUNTING AGAIN AND MEET A SAVAGE BUNNY.

Dumpy said, as he galloped out,
“I’m sure there are fierce wild beasts about!”
And the very next moment--wasn’t it funny?--
He was face to face with a savage Bunny!

[Illustration: Hobby Horse tied to a tree, Rag and Dumpy behind another tree, watch savage Bunny approach]

Do you think that he or Rag showed fear?
Certainly not. With a bark and a cheer,
They dashed on the foe with all their might,
And thrashed him soundly in one sharp fight.

The Fifth Adventure.

[Illustration: Golliwogg with legs crossed sits on the ground behind a tree]

THEY RESCUE A PRISONER AND TREAT HIM WITH MUCH KINDNESS.

Goodness! what can the matter be?
A poor black prisoner tied to a tree!
It shan’t be said that a boy and a dog
Wouldn’t rescue a Golliwogg!

[Illustration: Dumpy rides the hobby horse, spade in hand, with Rag close by, getting ready to save Golliwog who has been tied to a tree]

They freed the captive, they gave him food,
He was quite overcome with gratitude,
And they sang him, “Rule Britannia” then,
Over, and over, and over again.

The Sixth Adventure.

[Illustration: Rag pulls rope tied in a loop around the toy soldier]

THEY CAPTURE SIX OF THE ENEMY, WHO KICK.

When you meet with an enemy’s troop,
You get a rope and you make a loop,
You throw your lasso, and catch a man,
One by one--it’s a splendid plan!

[Illustration: Dumpy astride the hobby horse, with Rag in front, throws a lasso around one of three toy soldiers]

Dumpy tried it with great success,
Six fine soldiers he took--no less.
They kicked a little, as soldiers will,
But Rag said, “Grr!” if they didn’t keep still.

The Seventh Adventure.

[Illustration: Rag runs away from the scarecrow]

THEY FIGHT THE BATTLE OF BUNKERLOO.

A poppy-field they now did view--
’Twas the famous field of Bunkerloo.
The enemy, full of boast and brag,
Wanted to slaughter Dumpy and Rag.

[Illustration: Rag hides behind Dumpy’s legs as Dumpy with shield and sword approaches the scarecrow]

But Dumpy, giving his wildest yell,
With sword and shield on the foeman fell,--
And Rag, very brave, though he didn’t show it,
Cried, “Yap! yap! yap!” which meant, “Dumpy, go it!”

The Eighth Adventure.

[Illustration: Dumpy in feathered headdress pours medicine into a glass]

THEY GO INTO HOSPITAL, AND RAG’S WOUNDS ARE ATTENDED TO.

But Rag, poor dog, in the thick of the fight,
Got badly wounded--a piteous sight--
And Dumpy hoisted a small red flag,
While he was busy attending to Rag.

[Illustration: In the makeshift hospital, Rag sits atop the box, shaded by the umbrella, as Dumpy takes his temperature]

In a hospital tent Rag had to lie,
With a dismal whine and a bandaged eye.
But Dumpy said, “Though you’ve suffered loss,
I’m certain you’ll get the Victoria Cross!”

The Ninth Adventure.

[Illustration: Rag stands over one of the toy soldiers laying on the ground]

THEY CAPTURE A DESERTER.

The battle continued all round about,
And Dumpy ordered his sentries out;
But one of them thought he might get hurt,
So (very meanly) he tried to desert.

[Illustration: Dumpy sleeps on the ground, shaded by the umbrella, as Rag brings him the toy solider deserter]

The Captain was getting a moment’s nap,
Quite tired out with the fray, poor chap.
But Rag, with a face very stern and grim,
Spied the deserter and captured him.

The Tenth Adventure.

[Illustration: A toy solider leans on the jam jar]

THEY PUNISH THE DESERTER.

He had to be punished, of course he had;
But Dumpy hated to see him sad;
Dumpy said, with a frowning brow,
“We haven’t time for Court-martials now!

[Illustration: Dumpy sits over paperwork, feather and ink, as he declares punishment for the toy solider while Rag oversees the proceedings]

Just to show you how kind I am,
I shall only, Soldier, cut off your jam;
Back to your post! Whatever we do,
We must win the Battle of Bunkerloo!”

The Eleventh Adventure.

[Illustration: Rag stands on hind legs facing a toy solider]

THEY WIN THE BATTLE AND DUMPY IS PROMOTED.

When the evening drum went Rub-a-dub-dub,
Rag, like a General, mounted the tub.
And Captain Dumpy, the fight being done,
Came and reported how he had won.

[Illustration: Rag sits upright on a barrel, front paws holding the spade, as Dumpy salutes]

General Rag hadn’t much to say,
But he thought a good deal, for that was his way:
And what he meant when he said, “Bow-wow!”
Was--“You’re promoted to Colonel now!”

The Twelfth and last Adventure.

[Illustration: Dumpy feeds Rag from a large wooden spoon]

THEY BIVOUAC AFTER THE DAY’S WORK.

[Illustration: Dumpy sits beside the camp fire and stirs the stew in the pot as Rag waits with one ear cocked]

And, now as the enemies round about,
Were each defeated and put to rout,
And as he was safe from all attack,
Dumpy said, “Let us bivouac!
We’ll boil a kettle and make a stew,
And get some supper for me and you:
I think, for to-day, adventures are done,--
Rag, old boy! wasn’t it splendid fun!”
